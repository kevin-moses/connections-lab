Key idea: two things. (1) u_time is a uniform: it must be declared (`uniform float u_time;`) before use. (2) To move a pattern right by s, each pixel samples the pattern at x − s; here s = 0.1 × u_time, applied to uv.x before the × 20. Subtracting to move right is the counterintuitive part.
Common mistakes: using u_time without declaring it ("undeclared identifier 'u_time'"); declaring it as vec2 or int; `uv.x + u_time * 0.1` (slides left); `uv.x - u_time` (ten times too fast); `sin(uv.x * 20.0 - u_time * 0.1)` (subtracts after the * 20.0, so it moves 20× too slowly; `- u_time * 2.0` there is equivalent and correct); animating the color with sin(u_time) instead of moving the stripes.
Nudges: if the compile fails, ask what the error says and where uniforms are declared. For direction: ask which x in the starter's picture should end up at the middle at u_time = 2 (the prediction), then whether that x is bigger or smaller than the pixel's own. Have them inspect `x` or `wave` with time paused at a few values.
